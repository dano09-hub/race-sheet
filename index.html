How Clear and Runners work

Gap is the margin over the second-rated horse. Clear divides that gap by the spread of the field, so picks are comparable between races of any size. Runners is the field size, which tells you how hard the race is to win and whether the place terms run to three.

Where it went wrong

  • The rebalance was tested before it shipped. Form drops from 24 to 15, official rating rises from 10 to 15 and Topspeed from 8 to 12, and the whole set was re-run over these 21 races. Winners found stayed at 11, but the winner now sits in the top five 19 times instead of 18 and the top pick placed 15 times instead of 14. Heavier versions were worse, not better: rating 22, form 10 and Topspeed 18 found only 9 winners.
  • It is a favourite-follower. The sheet's top pick won 11 of 21 races; the starting-price favourite won 13. On today's evidence you would have done better backing the shortest price than following this model.
  • Every winner it missed was 5/1 or bigger — 5/1, 11/2, 17/2, 11/1 and 18/1 — and in two of those five it had the winner 6th or worse in the field (the others were 4th, 4th and 5th of their fields). The improving longshot is the blind spot.
  • The factors that would have found them are the ones it trusts least. Diamond Grace took the Market Rasen 15:01 at 17/2 scoring 93 of 100 on official rating and 89 on Topspeed, but only 33 on the rating factor and nothing from the tipsters. Even after the rebalance it was only 5th of 8: official rating now carries 15 of the weight and Topspeed 12, against 13 for the rating and 15 for form.
  • It ranks fields well and picks winners poorly. The winner was in its top three 16 times out of 21, and in its top five 19 times, but its top pick won only 11. The ordering near the head of a race is noisier than the ratings suggest.
  • A scoring bug was live in the morning sheet and is fixed. The tipster factor could go negative for horses nobody tipped (as low as −300), pushing unbacked runners down harder than intended. Correcting it reordered at least two races: total winners fell from 12 to 11, and at Thurles 15:15 the 18/1 winner Duke Otto dropped from 3rd to 8th. The corrected model is, if anything, more of a favourite-follower — so the verdict above is not flattered by the fix.
How the rating is calculated

Each factor is scored 0–100 and averaged with fixed weights. Nothing is user-adjustable, so every horse in every race is treated identically.

Market (18)
Implied probability from the price, scaled so the shortest-priced runner scores 100.
Form (15)
Last six finishing positions, most recent weighted heaviest, blended 65/35 with how often the horse actually hits the frame. No top-three finish in its last six caps it at 55; one run caps it at 68, two at 76; no form scores 30.
Tipsters (5)
How many of the card's tipsters have put the horse up, normalised across the field. Naps are shown in brackets next to the count but are not scored separately — a nap is one tipster's strongest view, not an extra vote.
Timeform (13)
Timeform's rating, normalised across the field — an independent view of the form it has actually produced. Cards built from Racing TV carry it; Sporting Life cards do not, and the factor drops out.
Official rating (15)
The handicapper's view of ability, normalised across the field.
Course & trip (10)
Whether it has won at the track and over the distance. A record like 1/3 is used as a rate; Y/N flags score 72/38.
Topspeed (12)
Speed figure, normalised across the field.
Going (4)
Whether the horse acts on today's surface. If nobody in the race is marked, the factor drops out rather than counting as neutral.
Fitness (3)
Days since last run: peak at 15–35, penalised for long absences and quick reappearances.
Weight (2)
Lower weight scores higher — the point of a handicap.
Stable form (3)
Trainer and jockey 30-day strike rates, straight from the card.

Missing data: if a factor has no figure for a horse, or is identical for the whole field, it is dropped and the other weights renormalise for that runner. A race with no prices is ranked without the market at all, and says so.

What this is not: a forecast. It has no sectional times, no video, no gallops reports and no stable information. It makes the same reasoning explicit and consistent for all 198 runners — it does not make it correct.
