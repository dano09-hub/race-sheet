How the ranking works

Score is the model’s 0–100 rating. Gap is the margin over the second-rated horse. Clear divides that gap by the spread of the field, so picks are comparable between races of any size. Factors is how many model inputs were available; thin cards are not treated as strong evidence.

Unmissable

The model’s absolute standouts: top picks that combine a high rating with a wide, dominant margin over their own field. These are the races worth a closer look before the off.

What makes a horse unmissable?

All five tests must pass: a score of 80 or higher, a gap of at least 15 points to the second-rated horse, a Clear figure of at least 30%, at least six factors available, and a field of at least five runners. Thin-data cards are excluded rather than promoted. This is still a rating, not a guarantee.

How the rating is calculated

Each factor is scored 0–100 and averaged with fixed weights. Nothing is user-adjustable, so every horse in every race is treated identically. The eleven GB/IE factors total 100; Draw adds 4 where a card carries it, so the full array has twelve entries and 104 nominal weight. The scorer divides by the active weight, so these are relative weights and the effective shares always total 100.

Market (18)
Implied probability from the price, scaled so the shortest-priced runner scores 100.
Form (15)
Last six finishing positions, most recent weighted heaviest, blended 65/35 with how often the horse actually hits the frame. No top-three finish in its last six caps it at 55; one run caps it at 68, two at 76; no form scores 30.
Tipsters (5)
How many of the card's tipsters have put the horse up, normalised across the field. Naps are shown in brackets next to the count but are not scored separately — a nap is one tipster's strongest view, not an extra vote.
Card rating (13)
The rating printed on the card — Racing Post Rating on horseracing.net cards, Timeform on Racing TV cards — normalised across the field. One rating product is used per race, never a mix of the two.
Official rating (15)
The handicapper's view of ability, normalised across the field.
Course & trip (10)
Whether it has won at the track and over the distance. A record like 1/3 is used as a rate; Y/N flags score 72/38.
Draw (4)
Post position; used where the card carries a draw (US/HK), absent on GB/IE cards so the factor drops.
Topspeed (12)
Speed figure, normalised across the field.
Won last time out (4)
Whether the horse won its latest start: Y scores 72, N scores 28. Cards that do not carry the flag drop the factor rather than guessing at the going.
Fitness (3)
Days since last run: peak at 15–35, penalised for long absences and quick reappearances.
Weight (2)
Lower weight scores higher — the point of a handicap.
Stable form (3)
Trainer and jockey 30-day strike rates, straight from the card.

Missing data: if a factor has no figure for a horse, or is identical for the whole field, it is dropped and the other weights renormalise for that runner. A race with no prices is ranked without the market at all, and says so.

What this is not: a forecast. It has no sectional times, no video, no gallops reports and no stable information. It applies the same reasoning consistently to every runner on the card — it does not make it correct.
